Compressing Chess Evaluation Networks for Edge-Device Inference

We compare four compression strategies — knowledge distillation, architecture reduction, weight pruning, and post-training quantization — to evaluate how each affects the accuracy–efficiency tradeoff for chess evaluation networks under edge-device compute constraints.

University of Washington · CSE · Spring 2026
Motivation

Why Compress?

Modern chess engines such as Leela Chess Zero (Lc0) rely on deep residual neural networks that demand significant compute and memory — competition-grade networks range from 160 to 330 MB and require 2–4 GB of GPU RAM for real-time evaluation. This makes them impractical for edge devices like smartphones and single-board computers with limited RAM.

We investigate which compression strategies yield the best tradeoff between evaluation accuracy and inference cost. Specifically, we compare four techniques — knowledge distillation, smaller architecture design, structured weight pruning, and post-training quantization — and whether they can be composed to let small models retain the performance of their larger counterparts.

Data

Datasets & Preprocessing

Positions are sampled from two large, open sources and labeled with evaluations from Stockfish (state-of-the-art conventional engine) as objective ground-truth targets.

  • Lichess Open Database — millions of game positions from real human play across all Elo ranges
  • Lumbra's Gigabase — high-quality curated OTB game database with broad coverage of openings, middlegames, and endgames

Each position is encoded as a multi-channel tensor representation of the 8×8 board (piece type, color, castling rights, en passant). Stockfish depth-20 evaluations provide centipawn labels; Lc0 Teacher logits provide soft targets for distillation.

Methodology

Compression Strategies

We systematically compare four well-defined compression techniques applied to a Leela Chess Zero (Lc0) baseline. Each strategy targets a different axis of cost reduction, and we evaluate them both independently and in combination.

StrategyTechniqueCost Axis
S1 · Distillation Train Student CNN on Lc0 soft logits + Stockfish hard labels Params, FLOPs
S2 · Smaller Arch Reduce width (F) & depth (N) of DW-Sep Conv backbone Params, FLOPs
S3 · Pruning Structured filter pruning (L1-norm) at 30%/50%/70% sparsity FLOPs, latency
S4 · Quantization Post-training FP32 → INT8 via PyTorch quantization API Memory, latency

Key comparison: each strategy is benchmarked in isolation, and the best-performing combinations (e.g., S1+S2+S4: distilled small model + INT8) are tested to identify compounding benefits on the Pareto frontier.

Student Architecture

The Student replaces standard convolutions with depthwise separable convolutions to drastically reduce FLOPs while preserving representational capacity.

LayerTypeOutput
Input Board tensor C × 8 × 8
Conv Block ×N DW-Sep Conv F × 8 × 8
SE Squeeze-Excite Channel attention F × 8 × 8
Policy Head 1×1 Conv + FC Move logits
Value Head 1×1 Conv + FC Scalar eval

Combined Distillation Loss

ℒ = α · MSE(ŷ, ySF) + (1−α) · KL( σ( zTτ ) ‖ σ( zSτ ) )

α balances hard-target (Stockfish) loss with soft-target (Teacher KL divergence). τ is distillation temperature.

Planned Experiments

  • S1 — Distillation ablation — compare hard-only (Stockfish MSE), soft-only (Lc0 KL), and combined loss to isolate distillation benefit
  • S2 — Architecture sweep — vary width F ∈ {32, 64, 128} and depth N ∈ {3, 6, 10, 15} to map capacity vs. cost
  • S3 — Pruning sweep — apply L1-norm structured filter pruning at 30%, 50%, and 70% sparsity with fine-tuning
  • S4 — Quantization — apply post-training FP32 → INT8 quantization and measure latency speedup vs. accuracy drop
  • Composition — combine best S1+S2+S3+S4 configurations and plot all variants on a unified Pareto frontier (MSE vs. latency, MSE vs. params)

Related Works

Key Prior Work

1. Hinton et al., "Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network" (2015)
Introduced knowledge distillation, training a small student network to mimic a large teacher's softened output distribution. This foundational technique underpins our S1 distillation strategy — we adopt the temperature-scaled KL divergence loss directly from this work.
2. Howard et al., "MobileNets: Efficient CNNs for Mobile Vision" (2017)
Proposed depthwise separable convolutions to dramatically reduce computation and model size for mobile deployment. Our S2 Student architecture directly adapts this factorization to the chess board tensor, replacing standard convolutions to achieve edge-viable FLOPs.
3. Li et al., "Pruning Filters for Efficient ConvNets" (ICLR 2017)
Demonstrated that ranking convolutional filters by L1-norm and removing the least important ones yields compact models with minimal accuracy loss after fine-tuning. We apply this structured pruning approach as our S3 strategy, sweeping sparsity levels to map the pruning–accuracy tradeoff.
